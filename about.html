CME Weekly is a weekly digest of new papers in computational materials science. Every Monday the past week's papers and preprints are collected, and a short list is chosen with a note of one or two sentences on each.

What is covered#

Computational materials science and engineering, plus experimental papers that establish a mechanism or test a computational design. Picks are grouped into eight threads:

  • Electrocatalysis & photocatalysis
  • Batteries & solid electrolytes
  • 2D materials: mechanics, defects, strain
  • High-entropy & nanostructured alloys
  • Architected materials & metamaterials
  • Machine learning for materials, which cuts across the other threads
  • Radiation & extreme environments
  • Composites & damage mechanics

Each issue also has a preprint watch for arXiv papers that are not yet peer-reviewed, and sometimes a few reviews worth reading.

How an issue is made#

  1. Gather. New papers from the past seven days are collected from OpenAlex and arXiv.
  2. Rank. Candidates are ranked by how closely they match the threads and by a predicted impact score.
  3. Select. A shortlist is read and the final picks are chosen, each with a short note.

A paper is picked for what it shows, not for its score alone. When a pick has a low or missing impact score, a Picked for badge says why it is in the issue.

Reading a card#

  • Tier places the journal in a broad band. It is context, not a verdict on the paper.
  • Impact meter shows the predicted chance that the paper ends up among the most-cited papers of its field and year. The impact model page explains it.
  • Impact on a preprint (dashed meter) compares its first and last authors' citations over the past three years with those of other recent arXiv preprints in the same topics. No meter appears when the last author's publication record cannot be found.
  • est. on a dashed meter marks an estimate made without venue information, used for unfamiliar journals.
  • Hidden gem marks a paper that scores well above what its venue alone would suggest.
  • Picked for gives the reason for a pick that its score does not explain.
  • Upvote lets readers mark papers they found useful, one vote per browser.

Sources#

Metadata and abstracts come from OpenAlex and arXiv. Every card links to the paper's DOI or arXiv page. The digest hosts no full texts.
