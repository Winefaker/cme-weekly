Correlated atomic force, electron and X-ray microscopy during oxygen evolution shows beta-Co(OH)2 nanoplates restructuring while leached Co re-forms as Co oxide nanoparticles on the carbon support, and that trace Fe in the electrolyte yields a more active bimetallic surface. Direct operando evidence on what the working catalyst actually is, which computational site models need to start from.
Open (opens in a new tab)

Water splitting is a crucial component in sustainable energy conversion, with the oxygen evolution half-cell reaction (OER) remaining a major challenge. Rationalizing the morphological aspects underlying OER activity is complicated by restructuring and chemical state changes occurring concurrently on the catalyst’s surfaces. Here, using correlated atomic force, electron and X-ray microscopy during OER, we show that the restructuring of β-Co(OH) 2 nanoplates takes place together with the formation of Co oxide nanoparticles on the carbon support from leached Co species. Adding small amounts of Fe to the electrolyte not only results in the formation of a more active bimetallic nanoplate surface; it also partially hinders Co dissolution and results in a lower density of redeposited CoO x nanoparticles. These results emphasize how pre-catalyst restructuring and dissolution–redeposition during OER can combine to create active sites, and how subtle variations in reaction conditions and electrolyte composition can lead to distinctive catalyst morphologies and reactivities.
