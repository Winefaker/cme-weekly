Tier AHigh-entropy & nanostructured alloys
Impact96 %Day-one model: probability of landing in the field's top 10 %
Group IV-V bcc refractory alloys show a plateau in yield strength and fracture toughness down to 20 K with no ductile-to-brittle transition, attributed to global twinning and omega-phase activation competing with screw-dislocation glide. Challenges the standard picture of bcc low-temperature embrittlement and gives targets for atomistic modelling.
Open (opens in a new tab)

Plastic deformation in body-centred cubic (bcc) metals and alloys is conventionally mediated by screw dislocations. Their temperature-sensitive motion is attributed to a steep rise in strength with decreasing temperature that constricts plastic zones at crack tips, leading to a ductile-to-brittle transition 1 . Here we evaluated the mechanical properties of group IV–V bcc refractory alloys with similar electronic structures at 20 K and found that these materials instead exhibit a plateau in tensile yield strength and fracture toughness as a function of temperature, with no apparent ductile-to-brittle transition. This behaviour arises from the global activation of twinning and an ω-phase transformation, which nucleate dislocations at interfaces before global dislocation motion. The initial mechanism is temperature insensitive and controls yielding, thereby preventing a crack-tip plastic zone from constricting with decreasing temperature.
