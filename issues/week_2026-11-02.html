Open (opens in a new tab)

The electrode potential of Li metal is fundamental to the performance and stability of Li-metal batteries, yet its accurate prediction across diverse electrolyte chemistries remains a challenge. Standard quantum chemistry using cluster models fails to account for explicit bulk solvation, while ab initio molecular dynamics is restricted by the high computational cost of thermodynamic sampling. In this work, we present an integrated framework that combines a universal machine learning potential for electrolytes with a data-efficient fine-tuning strategy to compute Li electrode potentials. This workflow achieves ab initio level accuracy using ∼200 extra density functional theory calculations per electrolyte system. Furthermore, the identified relationship between local Li+ solvation environments and electrode potential shifts provides mechanistic insights into electrolyte-dependent redox behavior and offers guidance for rational electrolyte design.

Impact93 %Preprint: the first and last authors' citations over the past three years are higher than those of 93 % of recent arXiv preprints in these topics
EAM and DFT calculations show unstable planar fault energies are far less temperature-sensitive than stacking-fault energies and relate them to shear modulus and Burgers vector, with deviations for intermetallics. Proposes how to estimate these fault energies in multicomponent alloys from pure elements.
Open (opens in a new tab)

The unstable planar fault energies (UPFEs) govern slip and twinning but are not experimentally measurable. Using EAM potentials complemented by DFT, we investigate their temperature and compositional dependence and their relationship with the shear modulus. For pure \textitfcc elements with low stacking fault energies (SFEs), UPFEs are substantially less temperature-sensitive than SFEs. The reported linear relationship between UPFEs and the product of shear modulus and Burgers vector (μbp) holds for close-packed elements and solid solutions, but deviates for intermetallics and non-close-packed elements. In the Co-Cr-Fe-Mn-Ni system, binary alloys show composition-dependent deviations, while the correlation strengthens with increasing chemical complexity. Furthermore, μbp of multicomponent alloys linearly correlates with the composition-weighted elemental μbp (R2=0.95), despite a systematic negative deviation. This provides a simple, computationally inexpensive route for estimating μbp and screening UPFEs directly from pure elemental constituents.

Collective dipole effects amplify field-driven ion transport in amorphous Li3PO4

arXiv·2 Nov 2026·Haruka Doyu … Satoshi Watanabe (3 authors)

arXiv preprintBatteries & solid electrolytesPicked for: collective dipole effect in amorphous electrolyte
Machine-learned potentials and a learned Born-effective-charge model show that collective dipole effects amplify field-driven Li transport in amorphous Li3PO4 compared with a vacancy crystal. Explains why disorder can help ion transport at high field.
Open (opens in a new tab)

Amorphous solid electrolytes often outperform their crystalline counterparts, but how disorder shapes ionic transport under strong electric fields is poorly understood. We use molecular dynamics with a machine-learned interatomic potential and a machine-learned Born-effective-charge model to measure Li mobility in amorphous Li3PO4 and in a crystal with Li vacancies, from the linear to the strongly nonlinear regime. The amorphous phase shows a much stronger field enhancement, and we trace this to a collective dipole effect. The migrating ion carries the same charge in both phases. Alone it would not make the amorphous phase respond more strongly. What makes the difference is the environment. In the amorphous network the surrounding atoms move with the hop. Their motion makes the work the field performs vary widely between hops. Since hopping rates grow exponentially with the work, the most strongly assisted hops can dominate the drift. Disorder thus enhances field-driven transport through the environment rather than the carrier itself, a mechanism that may operate in disordered ion conductors in general.
